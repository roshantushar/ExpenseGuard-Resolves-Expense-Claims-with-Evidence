# Global Expense Policy 2024

Document ID: D01
Region: GLOBAL
Effective: 2024-01-01 to 2024-12-31
Category: general

> Internal policy document of Northstar Analytics Group. Precedence rank 6 (lower rank overrides higher rank where documents differ).

## GEP24-1.1 - Purpose and scope

This policy governs reimbursement of business expenses incurred by employees of Northstar Analytics Group and its subsidiaries for transactions dated between 1 January 2024 and 31 December 2024. Transactions dated in another calendar year are governed by the policy version published for that year.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to delineate the temporal scope of the reimbursement policy, ensuring that employees understand which transactions are eligible for reimbursement based on the specified calendar year. This is crucial for maintaining clarity and consistency in the application of the policy across the organization. Typical business situations covered by this clause include travel expenses, office supplies, and other business-related expenditures incurred during the designated period. By clearly establishing the timeframe, the policy aids in the effective management of financial resources and ensures compliance with fiscal regulations.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause may arise from employees assuming that expenses incurred outside the specified calendar year are automatically eligible for reimbursement. It is essential to clarify that only transactions dated within the defined period are governed by this policy, while others must adhere to the version applicable to their respective years. This clause is conceptually linked to the overall expense management framework, as it sets the foundation for the validity of subsequent claims and reinforces the importance of adhering to established timelines in the reimbursement process.

## GEP24-1.2 - Business purpose

Every expense must have a clear business purpose connected to company activity. Personal benefit alone is not reimbursable.

Interpretive guidance: This clause emphasizes the necessity for a clear business purpose associated with each expense incurred by employees. The primary aim is to ensure that all reimbursements are directly tied to activities that benefit the company, thereby safeguarding corporate resources and promoting responsible spending. Typical business situations covered under this clause include travel for client meetings, expenses for professional development, and costs associated with business-related events. By requiring a clear business purpose, the policy fosters a culture of accountability and aligns employee expenditures with the company's strategic objectives.

A frequent misconception about this clause is the belief that any expense can be justified as long as it is vaguely related to business activities. However, the policy explicitly states that personal benefits alone do not qualify for reimbursement, which underscores the need for a direct connection to company activities. This clause relates closely to the documentation requirements outlined in the subsequent policy sections, as a well-defined business purpose must be supported by adequate documentation to ensure compliance and facilitate the approval process.

## GEP24-1.3 - Documentation

A claim must state the merchant, transaction date, currency, total amount and a description sufficient to determine the business purpose. Category policies may require additional fields; where a required field is absent the claim cannot be approved.

Interpretive guidance: The intent of this clause is to establish clear guidelines regarding the documentation required for expense claims. By specifying the necessary details, such as the merchant, transaction date, currency, total amount, and a description of the business purpose, this clause aims to streamline the reimbursement process and ensure that claims are transparent and verifiable. Typical business situations that this clause addresses include employees submitting claims for travel expenses, office supplies, or any other business-related purchases. The requirement for comprehensive documentation serves to protect both the employee and the organization by minimizing the risk of errors and facilitating timely reimbursements.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause is the assumption that minimal documentation suffices for claim approval. The policy clearly states that a claim lacking any required field cannot be approved, which highlights the importance of thoroughness in documentation. This clause is closely related to the submission window and late submission policies, as timely and complete documentation is essential for ensuring that claims are submitted within the established timeframe and can be processed without delay.

## GEP24-2.1 - Submission window

Claims must be submitted within 60 calendar days of the transaction date. A claim submitted later is rejected unless an approved exception exists.

Interpretive guidance: This clause serves to establish a clear timeline for the submission of expense claims, ensuring that employees are aware of the expectations regarding the timely reporting of expenses. By mandating that claims be submitted within a specified timeframe following the transaction date, the policy aims to promote efficiency in the reimbursement process and facilitate accurate financial reporting. Typical business situations covered by this clause include employees submitting claims for travel expenses or other business-related costs incurred during work-related activities. Adhering to the submission window is crucial for maintaining effective financial management and accountability within the organization.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause is the belief that claims can be submitted at any time without consequence. Employees may not realize that late submissions are subject to rejection unless an approved exception exists. This clause is conceptually linked to the late submission and outage relief policies, as both emphasize the importance of timely submissions and outline the implications of failing to meet the established deadlines. Understanding these interconnections is vital for employees to navigate the reimbursement process effectively.

## GEP24-2.2 - Late submission and outage relief

Late submission tiers are counted in calendar days from the transaction date to the submission date. Formally recorded Finance system outage relief is published by circular and applies only to transactions dated inside the stated outage window.

Interpretive guidance: This clause addresses the implications of late submissions of expense claims and provides a framework for understanding the consequences of not adhering to the established submission window. By outlining that late submissions are counted in calendar days and may be rejected unless an approved exception exists, the policy aims to encourage timely reporting and accountability among employees. Typical business situations covered include instances where employees may forget to submit claims or encounter unforeseen delays. This clause reinforces the importance of adhering to deadlines to ensure smooth reimbursement processes and accurate financial tracking.

A common misunderstanding surrounding this clause is the assumption that all late submissions will be automatically accepted or reviewed without consequence. Employees may not fully grasp that only claims submitted within the specified timeframe are eligible for consideration, and that exceptions are not guaranteed. This clause is conceptually related to the submission window policy, as both emphasize the significance of timely submissions and the potential ramifications of delays. Understanding these interrelated policies is essential for employees to navigate the reimbursement landscape effectively.

## GEP24-2.3 - Currency

Thresholds expressed in SGD equivalent are tested as set out in the Currency and FX Handling Policy. Ceilings expressed in a local currency are tested in that currency without conversion.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to clarify how currency thresholds are assessed within the context of the reimbursement policy. By specifying that thresholds expressed in a certain currency are tested according to the guidelines set out in the Currency and FX Handling Policy, the clause aims to ensure consistency and accuracy in the evaluation of claims. Typical business situations covered include employees submitting claims for expenses incurred in various currencies, such as international travel or purchases made abroad. The clear delineation of currency handling helps to mitigate confusion and ensures that all claims are processed fairly and uniformly.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause may arise from employees assuming that all currency conversions are automatically applied to claims. It is important to clarify that ceilings expressed in a local currency are tested in that currency without conversion, which can lead to discrepancies if not properly understood. This clause is conceptually linked to the broader financial management policies, as it emphasizes the importance of accurate currency handling in the reimbursement process and aligns with the organization's commitment to financial integrity.

## GEP24-3.1 - Order of precedence

Where rules differ, the order of precedence is: law and regulation; the applicable regional addendum; a Finance circular from its effective date, which amends the clause it names; an approved written exception within its stated scope; the category policy; this global policy; and finally definitions, frequently asked questions and worked examples, which never override the clauses above.

Interpretive guidance: The order of precedence clause establishes a clear hierarchy for resolving conflicts among various rules and guidelines governing corporate expenses. This clause is particularly relevant in situations where multiple policies might apply to a single transaction or expense claim. By delineating the order in which these rules should be considered, it ensures that employees and reviewers have a consistent framework to follow, thereby promoting compliance with legal and regulatory requirements as well as internal policies. This clarity helps to streamline decision-making processes and minimizes confusion in the administration of expense claims.

Common misunderstandings may arise regarding the interpretation of specific policies and how they interact with one another. Employees might assume that all policies hold equal weight, leading to potential conflicts in expense claims. It is essential to recognize that this clause is conceptually linked to other policy topics such as the applicability of circulars and the treatment of exceptions. Understanding the order of precedence helps clarify how to approach situations where multiple rules apply, ensuring that employees adhere to the most authoritative guidelines.

## GEP24-3.2 - Circulars

A Finance circular amends the clause it names from its effective date only. A transaction dated before the effective date is decided under the value that applied on its own transaction date.

Interpretive guidance: The circulars clause clarifies the temporal applicability of Finance circulars in relation to the expense policy. This provision is particularly significant when there are changes or updates to existing clauses, as it ensures that any amendments take effect only from the specified date of the circular. This promotes transparency and fairness in the application of rules, as it allows employees to understand which version of the policy applies to their transactions based on the date they occurred. This clause is essential in maintaining the integrity of the expense review process and ensuring compliance with updated standards.

A common misconception regarding this clause is the belief that circulars can retroactively alter the conditions of transactions that occurred prior to their effective date. This misunderstanding can lead to confusion among employees about which rules apply to their claims. This topic is closely related to the order of precedence, as understanding how circulars amend existing clauses helps clarify the hierarchy of rules. Employees should be aware that while circulars provide important updates, they do not change the rules governing transactions that have already occurred.

## GEP24-4.1 - Insufficient evidence

If a mandatory fact cannot be determined from the claim or from approved enterprise records, the reviewer requests the missing information from the employee. Facts must not be inferred from the employee narrative when an authoritative record exists.

Interpretive guidance: The insufficient evidence clause is designed to ensure that all claims submitted for reimbursement are supported by adequate documentation. This provision is particularly relevant in situations where the necessary information to substantiate a claim is missing or unclear. By mandating that reviewers request additional information from employees when required, the clause reinforces the importance of maintaining accurate and reliable records. This process not only protects the integrity of the expense review system but also ensures that employees are held accountable for providing comprehensive evidence to support their claims.

A frequent misunderstanding of this clause is the assumption that reviewers can make inferences based on the employee's narrative when authoritative records are available. This can lead to inconsistencies in the evaluation of claims and potentially result in improper reimbursements. This topic is conceptually linked to the human review clause, as both emphasize the importance of clear documentation and the role of reviewers in ensuring compliance with policy requirements. Understanding the necessity of authoritative evidence helps employees appreciate the rationale behind thorough documentation.

## GEP24-4.2 - Human review

A claim is escalated to Finance where two rules conflict and precedence cannot be determined from published text, where an approval conflicts with the transaction facts, or where the amount exceeds the Finance review threshold in the Approval Authority Policy.

Interpretive guidance: The human review clause establishes a protocol for escalating claims that present conflicts or uncertainties in the application of the expense policy. This is particularly crucial in situations where there is ambiguity in the interpretation of rules or discrepancies between approvals and transaction facts. By outlining the circumstances under which a claim should be escalated to Finance, this clause ensures that complex cases receive the appropriate level of scrutiny and that decisions are made in accordance with established guidelines, thereby enhancing the overall integrity of the expense management process.

Common misunderstandings may arise regarding what constitutes a conflict warranting escalation. Employees might not fully grasp the nuances of the policy, leading them to believe that any disagreement or uncertainty necessitates a review. This clause is conceptually related to the insufficient evidence clause, as both emphasize the importance of clarity and accuracy in expense claims. Understanding when to escalate a claim helps employees navigate the review process more effectively and ensures compliance with the overarching policy framework.

## GEP24-5.1 - Applicability by date

This policy version applies only to transactions dated in 2024. A later version does not apply retrospectively and an earlier version does not apply to a later transaction.

Interpretive guidance: The applicability by date clause delineates the specific temporal scope of the current version of the policy, making it clear that only transactions dated within the defined period are subject to its provisions. This is particularly important in managing expectations and ensuring that employees are aware of which policy version governs their claims. By establishing that later versions do not apply retrospectively, the clause protects employees from potential confusion and ensures that they adhere to the most relevant guidelines for their specific transactions, thereby promoting consistency in expense management.

A common misconception regarding this clause is the belief that earlier versions of the policy can still be applied to later transactions, leading to potential discrepancies in expense claims. This misunderstanding can create confusion among employees about which rules they should follow. This topic is closely related to the circulars clause, as both emphasize the importance of understanding the effective dates of policies and amendments. Recognizing the applicability of policy versions helps employees navigate the expense process more effectively and ensures compliance with the correct guidelines.

## GEP24-5.2 - Mixed expenses

A bill that combines personal and business items must be itemised. If the business amount cannot be separated from the personal amount, the claim is returned for the business amount rather than approved in full.

Interpretive guidance: The mixed expenses clause addresses the treatment of bills that contain both personal and business items, establishing a requirement for itemization. This provision is particularly relevant in situations where employees submit claims that do not clearly separate business-related expenses from personal ones. By mandating itemization, the clause ensures that only legitimate business expenses are reimbursed, thereby safeguarding the company's financial interests and promoting responsible spending practices among employees. This clarity helps to streamline the claims process and reinforces the importance of transparency in expense reporting.

A common misunderstanding related to this clause is the belief that a claim can be approved in full if it includes both personal and business expenses, provided the employee asserts that the business portion is valid. This can lead to improper reimbursements and potential compliance issues. This topic is conceptually linked to the insufficient evidence clause, as both emphasize the importance of clear documentation and the necessity of substantiating claims with appropriate evidence. Understanding the implications of mixed expenses helps employees navigate the claims process more effectively and ensures adherence to the policy.
