# Global Expense Policy 2026

Document ID: D03
Region: GLOBAL
Effective: 2026-01-01 to 2026-12-31
Category: general

> Internal policy document of Northstar Analytics Group. Precedence rank 6 (lower rank overrides higher rank where documents differ).

## GEP26-1.1 - Purpose and scope

This policy governs reimbursement of business expenses incurred by employees of Northstar Analytics Group and its subsidiaries for transactions dated between 1 January 2026 and 31 December 2026. Transactions dated in another calendar year are governed by the policy version published for that year.

Interpretive guidance: This clause delineates the purpose and scope of the reimbursement policy, specifically targeting business expenses incurred by employees during a defined calendar year. It provides clarity on which transactions are eligible for reimbursement, ensuring that employees are aware of the temporal limitations of the policy. This is particularly important in situations where employees may seek reimbursement for expenses incurred outside the specified timeframe, thereby preventing confusion and ensuring compliance with the governing policy for each year.

A common misunderstanding might involve the assumption that expenses incurred outside the specified dates can still be reimbursed under this policy. It is essential to understand that each year's policy is distinct and must be adhered to accordingly. This clause is related to other policy topics, such as documentation and submission requirements, as it underscores the need for employees to be aware of the temporal context of their expenses when seeking reimbursement.

## GEP26-1.2 - Business purpose

Every expense must have a specific, auditable business purpose. Generic wording such as business expense or client activity is insufficient where a category policy requires named attendees, a route, an event, or a project.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to ensure that every business expense claimed by employees is justifiable and can be audited effectively. By requiring a specific business purpose for each expense, the policy aims to eliminate vague or generic descriptions that may lead to misunderstandings or misuse of funds. This clause is particularly relevant in scenarios where expenses could be scrutinized, such as during audits or when justifying expenditures to stakeholders.

A common misunderstanding may arise from the belief that any mention of a business context suffices for expense justification. However, generic terms like 'business expense' do not meet the standards set forth in this clause. This topic is conceptually linked to documentation requirements, as both emphasize the need for clarity and specificity in expense reporting, thereby fostering accountability and transparency in financial practices.

## GEP26-1.3 - Documentation

A claim must state the merchant, transaction date, currency, total amount and a description sufficient to determine the business purpose. Category policies may require additional fields; where a required field is absent the claim cannot be approved.

Interpretive guidance: This clause outlines the documentation requirements necessary for the approval of expense claims. By specifying the essential details that must be included, such as merchant information and transaction descriptions, the policy aims to facilitate a thorough review process. This is particularly important in business scenarios where the legitimacy of expenses needs to be verified, ensuring that all claims can be traced back to a valid business purpose.

Common misunderstandings may occur when employees submit claims that lack the required details, believing that their narrative alone suffices for approval. It is crucial to recognize that missing information can lead to claim denial. This clause is closely related to the business purpose requirement, as both emphasize the importance of detailed and accurate documentation in supporting the legitimacy of expenses.

## GEP26-2.1 - Submission window

Claims must be submitted within 45 calendar days of the transaction date. A claim submitted from day 46 up to and including day 75 requires manager approval. A claim submitted after day 75 is escalated to Finance.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish a clear timeline for the submission of expense claims, thereby promoting timely processing and accountability. By setting specific submission windows, the policy aims to streamline the reimbursement process and ensure that claims are handled efficiently. This is particularly relevant in business situations where delays in submission could affect cash flow or financial reporting.

A common misunderstanding might be the belief that claims can be submitted at any time without consequence. However, this clause clearly outlines the consequences of late submissions, which can lead to additional approval requirements. This topic is conceptually linked to the late submission and outage relief clause, as both address the importance of adhering to timelines and the implications of failing to do so.

## GEP26-2.2 - Late submission and outage relief

Late submission tiers are counted in calendar days from the transaction date to the submission date. Formally recorded Finance system outage relief is published by circular and applies only to transactions dated inside the stated outage window.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to provide clarity on the treatment of late submissions and the circumstances under which relief may be granted due to system outages. By defining the parameters for late submissions, the policy aims to maintain a fair and consistent approach to expense claims. This is particularly important in situations where employees may be affected by unforeseen technical issues, ensuring that they are not unduly penalized for circumstances beyond their control.

A common misunderstanding may involve the assumption that all late submissions are treated equally, without regard for the context of the delay. However, this clause specifies that only formally recorded outages will be considered for relief. This topic is related to submission windows, as both emphasize the importance of timely claims while also recognizing the need for flexibility in certain situations.

## GEP26-2.3 - Currency

Thresholds expressed in SGD equivalent are tested as set out in the Currency and FX Handling Policy. Ceilings expressed in a local currency are tested in that currency without conversion.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish clear guidelines for the evaluation of financial thresholds, ensuring that they are tested appropriately in the relevant currency. This is particularly important in international transactions where currency fluctuations can significantly impact financial reporting and compliance. The clause is designed to cover situations where expenses or financial limits are expressed in different currencies, thus providing a framework for consistency and accuracy in financial assessments.

A common misunderstanding surrounding this clause is the belief that all financial thresholds can be converted into a single currency for evaluation purposes. However, the clause emphasizes that ceilings expressed in local currencies must be assessed without conversion, which can lead to confusion in multi-currency environments. This topic is conceptually linked to the Currency and FX Handling Policy, as both address the handling of different currencies in financial transactions, ensuring that employees understand the importance of adhering to the specified guidelines.

## GEP26-3.1 - Order of precedence

Where rules differ, the order of precedence is: law and regulation; the applicable regional addendum; a Finance circular from its effective date, which amends the clause it names; an approved written exception within its stated scope; the category policy; this global policy; and finally definitions, frequently asked questions and worked examples, which never override the clauses above.

Interpretive guidance: This clause serves to clarify the hierarchy of rules applicable within the organization, ensuring that employees understand which regulations take precedence in situations where discrepancies arise. It is essential for maintaining compliance and consistency across various policies and regulations, particularly in complex scenarios involving legal requirements, regional adaptations, and internal directives. By establishing a clear order of precedence, the clause helps mitigate confusion and potential conflicts in decision-making processes.

A frequent misconception is that all policies hold equal weight, leading to potential conflicts in interpretation. This clause makes it clear that certain rules, such as laws and regulations, take precedence over others, which can be vital in ensuring compliance with external requirements. This topic is closely related to the concept of policy governance, as it underscores the importance of understanding the relationships between different policy documents and their implications for operational decisions.

## GEP26-3.2 - Circulars

A Finance circular amends the clause it names from its effective date only. A transaction dated before the effective date is decided under the value that applied on its own transaction date.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to delineate the effective date of Finance circulars and their impact on previously dated transactions. This is crucial for ensuring that employees apply the correct version of a policy when processing transactions, thereby maintaining compliance and accuracy in financial reporting. By specifying that amendments take effect only from their stated date, the clause helps to prevent confusion and ensures that all transactions are evaluated based on the rules that were in effect at the time of their occurrence.

A common misunderstanding is that circulars can retroactively affect transactions that occurred prior to their effective date. This clause clarifies that only transactions dated on or after the effective date are subject to the amendments, which is essential for maintaining the integrity of financial records. This topic is related to the broader theme of policy updates and their implications, emphasizing the need for employees to stay informed about changes in policy and how they apply to ongoing transactions.

## GEP26-4.1 - Insufficient evidence

If a mandatory fact cannot be determined from the claim or from approved enterprise records, the reviewer requests the missing information from the employee. Facts must not be inferred from the employee narrative when an authoritative record exists.

Interpretive guidance: This clause is designed to ensure that all claims submitted for review are substantiated by adequate evidence, thereby promoting transparency and accountability within the expense reporting process. In situations where mandatory facts are missing, the reviewer is empowered to request additional information from the employee, ensuring that claims are thoroughly vetted before approval. This process is vital for maintaining the integrity of financial records and ensuring compliance with internal policies.

A common misconception is that the reviewer can make assumptions based on the employee's narrative when authoritative records are available. The clause explicitly states that facts must not be inferred, which can lead to misunderstandings regarding the review process. This topic is conceptually linked to the principles of evidence-based decision-making and the importance of maintaining accurate records, reinforcing the need for thorough documentation in all financial transactions.

## GEP26-4.2 - Human review

A claim is escalated to Finance where two rules conflict and precedence cannot be determined from published text, where an approval conflicts with the transaction facts, or where the amount exceeds the Finance review threshold in the Approval Authority Policy.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish a protocol for escalating claims to the Finance department when conflicts arise that cannot be resolved through the existing policy framework. By outlining specific scenarios that trigger this escalation, the clause ensures that complex cases receive appropriate attention and expertise, thereby safeguarding the integrity of the expense approval process. This is particularly important in situations where conflicting rules or approvals could lead to financial discrepancies.

A common misunderstanding is that all claims can be resolved at the initial review level, without recognizing the need for escalation in more complex cases. This clause clarifies that certain situations necessitate a higher level of scrutiny, which is essential for maintaining compliance and accuracy. This topic is closely related to the Approval Authority Policy, as it highlights the importance of understanding the thresholds and conditions under which claims should be escalated for further review.

## GEP26-5.1 - Applicability by date

This policy version applies only to transactions dated in 2026. A later version does not apply retrospectively and an earlier version does not apply to a later transaction.

Interpretive guidance: This clause serves to define the applicability of the current version of the policy, ensuring that employees understand which transactions are governed by the specified guidelines. By clearly stating that this version applies only to transactions dated in a specific year, the clause helps to maintain clarity and consistency in the application of policy rules. This is particularly important in environments where multiple versions of a policy may exist, as it prevents confusion regarding which rules are applicable to specific transactions.

This topic is related to policy lifecycle management, emphasizing the importance of keeping employees informed about the most current policies and ensuring that they understand the implications of policy changes on their transactions.

## GEP26-5.2 - Mixed expenses

A bill that combines personal and business items must be itemised. If the business amount cannot be separated from the personal amount, the claim is returned for the business amount rather than approved in full.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to ensure clarity and accountability in expense reporting by requiring itemisation of bills that contain both personal and business-related items. This is particularly relevant in situations where employees may incur mixed expenses during business activities, such as dining or travel, where personal and professional expenditures are intertwined. By mandating itemisation, the company aims to facilitate accurate expense tracking and reimbursement processes, thus safeguarding corporate resources and maintaining compliance with financial policies.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause is the belief that any mixed expense can be fully reimbursed if the employee asserts it was primarily for business purposes. However, the requirement for itemisation clarifies that only the business-related portion is eligible for reimbursement. This clause is conceptually linked to other policies regarding expense eligibility and reimbursement procedures, as it underscores the necessity for clear delineation between personal and business expenses, which is critical for maintaining financial integrity within the organisation.

## GEP26-6.1 - Evidence standard

A decision must cite the applicable clause and every enterprise fact that the clause requires. The travel request is the authoritative source for trip approval and destination; employee narrative never overrides a system record.

Interpretive guidance: This clause establishes the evidence standard necessary for making decisions regarding expense claims and travel requests. It is designed to ensure that all decisions are grounded in documented facts and that the travel request is the primary source of authority for trip approvals. This is particularly important in situations where discrepancies may arise, as it provides a clear framework for validating claims and ensuring that all relevant information is considered, thereby promoting fairness and consistency in the decision-making process.

A common misconception is that employee narratives or explanations can override the established evidence requirements. In reality, the clause clarifies that only documented travel requests can substantiate approvals, thereby reinforcing the importance of accurate record-keeping. This principle is closely related to the broader context of compliance and governance within the organisation, as it ensures that all financial transactions are supported by verifiable evidence, which is critical for audits and financial reporting.

## GEP26-6.2 - Automation boundary

Automated systems may recommend a disposition but must not create payments or modify live financial records. All automated recommendations are logged with the evidence relied on.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to delineate the boundaries of automated systems in the context of financial transactions. It specifies that while automated systems may provide recommendations regarding expense dispositions, they lack the authority to execute payments or alter live financial records. This is particularly relevant in scenarios where efficiency and accuracy are paramount, as it allows for streamlined processes while ensuring that human oversight remains integral to financial decision-making.

A frequent misunderstanding is that automated recommendations can be treated as definitive approvals for financial transactions. However, this clause clarifies that such recommendations are merely advisory and do not replace the need for human intervention in executing financial actions. This topic intersects with related policies on internal controls and financial governance, as it highlights the importance of maintaining a clear separation between automated processes and human oversight to mitigate risks associated with financial inaccuracies.
