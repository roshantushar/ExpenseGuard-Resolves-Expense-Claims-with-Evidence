# Currency and FX Handling Policy

Document ID: D16

Effective: 2024-01-01 to 2099-12-31

## FX-1.1 - SGD equivalent

An amount in another currency is converted to SGD using the monthly rate in the FX rate table for the calendar month of the transaction date. The converted amount is not rounded before it is compared with a threshold.

Interpretive guidance: This clause outlines the process for converting expenses incurred in foreign currencies to the local currency for reporting and approval purposes. The purpose is to ensure that all expenses are accurately reflected in the company's financial records in a consistent manner. Typical business scenarios include international travel or purchases made by employees in foreign markets, where transactions may be recorded in various currencies and need to be standardized for effective financial management.

A common misunderstanding is that the conversion process may involve rounding or adjustments before comparing amounts to thresholds, which is not permitted under this clause. The emphasis on using the monthly rate directly for conversions ensures clarity and consistency in financial reporting. This topic is related to other policies concerning currency management and expense reporting, as it underscores the importance of accurate currency conversion in maintaining the integrity of financial data across different regions and currencies.

## FX-1.2 - Which tests use SGD

Approval thresholds expressed in SGD equivalent, software tiers and equipment tiers are tested on the SGD equivalent. Regional ceilings and regional approval thresholds expressed in a local currency are tested in that currency.

Interpretive guidance: This clause specifies the circumstances under which expenses are tested against approval thresholds, distinguishing between those expressed in the local currency and those converted to the local currency equivalent. The purpose is to provide clarity on how different currencies are treated within the approval framework, ensuring that all expenses are evaluated fairly and consistently. Typical scenarios include regional transactions where local currencies are used, necessitating careful consideration of how these amounts relate to corporate approval processes.

A common misunderstanding is that all expenses should be converted to the local currency for evaluation, regardless of the context, which can lead to confusion. This clause clarifies that certain regional thresholds remain in their original currency, thereby maintaining the integrity of local financial practices. This topic is conceptually linked to other policies regarding currency conversion and expense approval, emphasizing the importance of context in financial decision-making and compliance with corporate governance.

## FX-1.3 - Comparison

A threshold is crossed when the amount is strictly greater than the threshold. An amount equal to the threshold does not cross it.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish a clear criterion for determining when a specified threshold has been crossed in relation to expense amounts. This is particularly relevant in situations where approvals or additional scrutiny are required once certain financial limits are surpassed. It ensures that all employees understand that only amounts exceeding the threshold will trigger any necessary actions, thereby promoting clarity and consistency in expense reporting.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause is the belief that amounts equal to the threshold may also be considered as having crossed it. This can lead to confusion in reporting and compliance. It is important to note that this concept is closely related to other policy topics such as approval processes and reporting requirements, where precise definitions of thresholds are critical to maintaining financial discipline.

## FX-1.4 - Card-posted amounts

The amount posted on a corporate card statement is not used for threshold tests.

Interpretive guidance: This clause clarifies that the amounts reflected on corporate card statements are not utilized for the purpose of threshold assessments within the expense reporting framework. It serves to delineate the specific context in which these amounts are considered, ensuring that employees understand that the posted figures do not influence any evaluations related to compliance with expense limits or thresholds. This is particularly relevant in situations where employees may be submitting expenses that involve corporate card transactions, as it highlights the distinction between recorded amounts and actual expenses incurred.

A common misunderstanding may arise concerning the interpretation of posted amounts as indicators of allowable expenses. Employees might mistakenly believe that the amounts on their corporate card statements should be factored into their overall expense reporting, which is not the case. This clause is conceptually linked to broader policies concerning expense reporting and compliance, as it underscores the importance of accurately categorizing and reporting expenses without relying on potentially misleading figures from card statements.
