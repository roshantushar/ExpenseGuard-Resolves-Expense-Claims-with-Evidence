# Software, Subscriptions and Equipment Policy

Document ID: D13
Region: GLOBAL
Effective: 2024-01-01 to 2099-12-31
Category: software

> Internal policy document of Northstar Analytics Group. Precedence rank 5 (lower rank overrides higher rank where documents differ).

## SWE-1.1 - Subscriptions

A software or online service subscription requires a named business owner on the claim. Up to SGD 500 equivalent no approval is required. Above it, manager approval of type SOFTWARE or GENERAL is required.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish clear guidelines for the approval of software or online service subscriptions, ensuring that each subscription has a designated business owner responsible for its oversight. This is particularly important in situations where subscriptions are essential for daily operations or project execution. By requiring a named business owner, the organization promotes accountability and ensures that resources are allocated effectively, aligning with strategic business objectives.

A common misconception may be that any subscription can be claimed without the need for a business owner, leading to potential misuse or misallocation of resources. Furthermore, this clause intersects with other procurement policies that govern the acquisition of goods and services, highlighting the importance of oversight in managing subscriptions. Understanding the relationship between these policies can enhance compliance and ensure that all subscriptions contribute positively to the organization's operational efficiency.

## SWE-1.2 - Larger subscriptions

From 1 February 2026 a subscription above SGD 1,000 equivalent requires manager approval, an ACTIVE project record and an OPEN cost centre. If the project is not active or the cost centre is not open, the claim is escalated.

Interpretive guidance: This clause outlines the requirements for managing larger software subscriptions, particularly emphasizing the need for managerial approval, an active project record, and an open cost centre. It is designed to ensure that significant financial commitments are justified and linked to ongoing projects, thereby safeguarding the organization’s financial resources. This is especially pertinent in scenarios where the subscription is critical to a project's success and requires careful consideration before approval.

A frequent misunderstanding may occur regarding the distinction between general subscriptions and those that fall into this larger category, leading to confusion about the approval process. This clause is closely related to the previous one, as both address subscription management but differ in the scale and associated requirements. Understanding these distinctions is vital for compliance and effective financial management within the organization.

## SWE-1.3 - Prepaid annual plans

A prepaid annual plan above SGD 3,000 equivalent must be procured through Finance and the claim is escalated.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to regulate the procurement process for prepaid annual plans that exceed a defined financial threshold, mandating that such acquisitions be handled through the Finance department. This ensures that the organization maintains control over significant expenditures and that these investments are appropriately vetted. Such a requirement is particularly relevant for large-scale software solutions that can have a substantial impact on operational capabilities and budgetary considerations.

A common misconception may be that all prepaid plans can be procured independently without Finance's involvement, which could lead to potential financial mismanagement. This clause is interconnected with broader procurement and financial policies that govern spending and resource allocation, emphasizing the necessity of centralized oversight for significant financial commitments. Recognizing this relationship helps ensure adherence to organizational standards and promotes fiscal responsibility.

## SWE-2.1 - Equipment

Equipment up to SGD 300 equivalent is reimbursable. Equipment above that and up to SGD 1,000 equivalent requires manager approval. Equipment above SGD 1,000 equivalent is a capital asset and must be procured through Finance; the reimbursement claim is rejected.

Interpretive guidance: This clause delineates the reimbursement framework for equipment expenses, establishing clear thresholds for approval based on the cost of the equipment. It is designed to ensure that employees are aware of the financial parameters within which they can operate when acquiring equipment for business purposes. Typical situations covered under this clause include employees purchasing office supplies, tools, or technology required for their roles, where the cost of such items falls within specified ranges. The clause also clarifies the distinction between reimbursable expenses and capital assets, thereby streamlining the procurement process and ensuring compliance with financial regulations.

Common misunderstandings regarding this clause often arise from confusion about the thresholds for reimbursement and the categorization of equipment versus capital assets. Employees may mistakenly believe that any equipment purchase qualifies for reimbursement, regardless of its cost or intended use. This clause is conceptually related to the procurement policies, as it emphasizes the importance of following established financial procedures for asset acquisition. Additionally, it intersects with the personal devices policy, as it highlights the necessity for equipment to be directly related to business operations to qualify for reimbursement.

## SWE-2.2 - Personal devices

Devices primarily for personal use are not reimbursable.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to clearly state that devices intended primarily for personal use do not qualify for reimbursement under the corporate expense policy. This distinction is crucial to ensure that company funds are utilized solely for business-related expenses, thereby safeguarding the organization’s financial integrity. Typical scenarios covered by this clause include employees seeking reimbursement for personal smartphones, tablets, or other devices that are not essential for their job functions. By setting this boundary, the policy helps to prevent misuse of company resources and promotes responsible spending among employees.

A common misunderstanding related to this clause is the belief that devices with some business use can be reimbursed, even if they are primarily for personal use. Employees may argue that their personal devices are occasionally used for work purposes, but this does not meet the criteria for reimbursement as outlined in this clause. This topic is closely related to the equipment reimbursement policy, as it reinforces the principle that only purchases directly related to business needs are eligible for reimbursement. Understanding this distinction helps employees navigate the complexities of expense claims and maintain compliance with corporate financial policies.
