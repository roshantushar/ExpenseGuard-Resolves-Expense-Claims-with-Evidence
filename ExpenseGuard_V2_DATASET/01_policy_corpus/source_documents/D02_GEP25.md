# Global Expense Policy 2025

Document ID: D02
Region: GLOBAL
Effective: 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31
Category: general

> Internal policy document of Northstar Analytics Group. Precedence rank 6 (lower rank overrides higher rank where documents differ).

## GEP25-1.1 - Purpose and scope

This policy governs reimbursement of business expenses incurred by employees of Northstar Analytics Group and its subsidiaries for transactions dated between 1 January 2025 and 31 December 2025. Transactions dated in another calendar year are governed by the policy version published for that year.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to delineate the temporal framework within which employees of Northstar Analytics Group and its subsidiaries may seek reimbursement for business expenses. This policy specifically governs transactions that occur within the designated calendar year, ensuring that all claims are processed under the relevant guidelines applicable to that timeframe. Typical business situations covered by this clause include travel expenses, office supplies, and other operational costs incurred by employees while performing their duties, thereby aligning with the company's financial accountability and operational efficiency.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause is the belief that expenses incurred outside the specified calendar year can be submitted for reimbursement under the same policy. In reality, such expenses must adhere to the guidelines of the policy version applicable to their respective years, which may differ in terms of requirements and permissible expenses. This clause is conceptually related to the documentation and submission requirements, as it emphasizes the importance of adhering to the correct policy version, which is essential for ensuring compliance with the overall expense management framework.

## GEP25-1.2 - Business purpose

Expenses must primarily support company business. Mixed personal and business expenses require itemisation and only the documented business portion may be considered.

Interpretive guidance: This clause serves to establish that expenses must primarily support the business objectives of Northstar Analytics Group. It is intended to ensure that financial resources are allocated effectively and responsibly, thereby promoting a culture of fiscal prudence within the organization. Typical situations include travel for client meetings, training sessions, or other business-related activities, where employees may incur costs that are directly tied to their professional responsibilities. The emphasis on itemization for mixed personal and business expenses further underscores the need for transparency and accountability in financial reporting.

A frequent misconception is that any expense incurred during the course of work is automatically eligible for reimbursement. However, the requirement for expenses to primarily support business activities means that personal expenditures, even when mixed with business purposes, must be clearly delineated and justified. This clause is closely related to the documentation requirements, as both emphasize the necessity of providing clear and sufficient information to substantiate claims, ensuring that the company maintains a rigorous approach to expense management.

## GEP25-1.3 - Documentation

A claim must state the merchant, transaction date, currency, total amount and a description sufficient to determine the business purpose. Category policies may require additional fields; where a required field is absent the claim cannot be approved.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to outline the necessary documentation required for submitting expense claims, ensuring that all submissions are complete and verifiable. By mandating specific fields such as merchant name, transaction date, and a description of the business purpose, this clause aims to facilitate a streamlined review process, thereby enhancing operational efficiency. Typical business situations include the submission of claims for travel expenses, office supplies, or client entertainment, where detailed information is crucial for assessing the validity of the expense.

One common misunderstanding is the belief that any claim can be submitted without adhering to the specified documentation requirements. In reality, the absence of required fields will result in the inability to approve the claim, which can lead to delays and frustration for the employee. This clause is conceptually linked to the submission window and late submission policies, as both highlight the importance of timely and accurate submissions, reinforcing the company's commitment to maintaining a structured and accountable expense management system.

## GEP25-2.1 - Submission window

Claims must be submitted within 45 calendar days of the transaction date. A claim submitted from day 46 up to and including day 90 requires manager approval. A claim submitted after day 90 is escalated to Finance.

Interpretive guidance: This clause establishes the timeline within which employees must submit their expense claims for reimbursement, thereby promoting timely reporting and accountability. By setting a clear submission window, the policy aims to facilitate efficient processing of claims, ensuring that the financial records remain up-to-date and accurate. Typical business situations that this clause addresses include travel reimbursements, training expenses, and other operational costs that employees may incur, emphasizing the importance of adhering to the specified timeframe for submission.

A common misconception is that claims can be submitted at any time without consequence, leading to potential delays in reimbursement. However, the stipulation that claims submitted beyond the initial submission window require additional manager approval underscores the necessity of adhering to the established timelines. This clause is interconnected with the late submission policy, as both highlight the importance of timely submissions and the potential ramifications of failing to comply with the established deadlines, thereby reinforcing the company's commitment to efficient financial management.

## GEP25-2.2 - Late submission and outage relief

Late submission tiers are counted in calendar days from the transaction date to the submission date. Formally recorded Finance system outage relief is published by circular and applies only to transactions dated inside the stated outage window.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to provide clarity regarding the handling of late submissions and the relief available in the event of formally recorded Finance system outages. By outlining the tiers for late submissions, the policy aims to establish a fair and transparent process for employees who may face challenges in submitting their claims on time. Typical business situations include unforeseen circumstances that may prevent timely submission, such as technical issues or emergencies, thereby ensuring that employees are not unduly penalized in such cases.

A frequent misunderstanding is that all late submissions can be easily justified without adhering to the outlined tiers. In reality, the policy specifies that claims submitted beyond the initial window require manager approval, and those submitted after a certain period are escalated to Finance, which may complicate the approval process. This clause is conceptually related to the submission window policy, as both emphasize the importance of adhering to timelines while providing a structured approach to managing exceptions, thereby ensuring that the company maintains a robust expense management framework.

## GEP25-2.3 - Currency

Thresholds expressed in SGD equivalent are tested as set out in the Currency and FX Handling Policy. Ceilings expressed in a local currency are tested in that currency without conversion.

Interpretive guidance: This clause serves to clarify the treatment of thresholds related to currency conversions within the context of the company's expense policies. It is particularly relevant in situations where expenses may be incurred in various currencies, necessitating a consistent approach to evaluating these expenses against established ceilings. By specifying the handling of thresholds in both the local currency and its equivalent in another currency, the clause ensures that all employees understand the parameters for expense reporting and compliance, particularly when engaging in international business transactions or when expenses are incurred during travel.

A common misunderstanding related to this clause may arise from the interpretation of how conversions should be applied to different currencies. Employees may mistakenly believe that all expenses must be converted to a single currency for evaluation, which is not the case when local currency ceilings are applicable. This topic is closely related to the Currency and FX Handling Policy, which provides further guidance on the processes for currency conversion and the management of foreign exchange risks. Understanding the distinctions between local and converted thresholds is essential for accurate expense reporting and compliance.

## GEP25-3.1 - Order of precedence

Where rules differ, the order of precedence is: law and regulation; the applicable regional addendum; a Finance circular from its effective date, which amends the clause it names; an approved written exception within its stated scope; the category policy; this global policy; and finally definitions, frequently asked questions and worked examples, which never override the clauses above.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish a clear hierarchy of rules that govern the interpretation and application of the company's expense policies. In situations where multiple rules may apply or when there is ambiguity in the policy language, this order of precedence ensures that decisions are made consistently and in accordance with the highest authority. This is particularly relevant in complex business scenarios where regional addenda or specific Finance circulars may introduce variations to the global policy, thereby necessitating a structured approach to resolve conflicts and guide decision-making.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause is the belief that all documents carry equal weight in decision-making. In reality, the hierarchy outlined here emphasizes that legal and regulatory requirements take precedence over internal policies and guidelines. This clause is closely related to the concept of circulars, as it highlights the importance of understanding the effective date of amendments and how they interact with existing policies. Furthermore, it underscores the necessity of adhering to the defined order of precedence when interpreting conflicting rules, thereby ensuring compliance and uniformity in policy application.

## GEP25-3.2 - Circulars

A Finance circular amends the clause it names from its effective date only. A transaction dated before the effective date is decided under the value that applied on its own transaction date.

Interpretive guidance: This clause clarifies that a Finance circular modifies the specific clause it references from the date it becomes effective, ensuring that any transactions dated prior to this effective date are governed by the rules in place at that time. This is essential for maintaining consistency and fairness in the treatment of transactions, especially in dynamic business environments where policies may evolve. In practice, this clause is particularly relevant for employees submitting claims or engaging in financial transactions that may be affected by recent policy changes, as it delineates the timeframe for which each set of rules applies.

A frequent misconception is that circulars retroactively apply to transactions dated before their effective date, which is not the case according to this clause. It is crucial to understand that the effective date is a key factor in determining which rules govern a transaction. This topic is conceptually linked to the order of precedence, as both clauses work together to clarify the application of rules and the importance of timing in policy enforcement. Misinterpretation of these clauses can lead to disputes or confusion regarding the validity of claims, highlighting the need for careful attention to detail in financial documentation.

## GEP25-4.1 - Insufficient evidence

If a mandatory fact cannot be determined from the claim or from approved enterprise records, the reviewer requests the missing information from the employee. Facts must not be inferred from the employee narrative when an authoritative record exists.

Interpretive guidance: This clause serves the purpose of ensuring that all claims submitted for reimbursement are supported by verifiable evidence. In situations where mandatory facts are absent from the claim or cannot be substantiated by existing enterprise records, it is the responsibility of the reviewer to seek clarification from the employee. This process is vital for maintaining the integrity of the expense reporting system and ensuring that all claims are legitimate and accurately reflect business expenditures, thereby protecting the company from potential financial discrepancies.

A common misunderstanding surrounding this clause is the assumption that employee narratives can be used as sufficient evidence in the absence of authoritative records. However, this clause explicitly states that facts must not be inferred from narratives when reliable documentation is available. This topic is closely related to the human review process, as both clauses emphasize the importance of thorough verification and adherence to established guidelines when assessing claims. Proper documentation is essential not only for compliance but also for fostering a culture of accountability within the organization.

## GEP25-4.2 - Human review

A claim is escalated to Finance where two rules conflict and precedence cannot be determined from published text, where an approval conflicts with the transaction facts, or where the amount exceeds the Finance review threshold in the Approval Authority Policy.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to outline the circumstances under which a claim must be escalated to the Finance department for further review. This escalation occurs when there are conflicting rules, discrepancies between approvals and transaction facts, or when the claim exceeds a specified threshold. This process is crucial for ensuring that all claims are evaluated with the appropriate level of scrutiny, particularly in complex cases where standard procedures may not provide clear guidance. By establishing a formal review process, the company aims to uphold the integrity of its financial practices and ensure compliance with its policies.

A common misunderstanding is that all claims should automatically be escalated whenever there is a conflict or ambiguity, which is not the intent of this clause. Escalation is reserved for specific scenarios as defined, and not all conflicts warrant this level of intervention. This clause is conceptually linked to the principles of insufficient evidence and the order of precedence, as both emphasize the importance of clarity and adherence to established processes in the review of claims. Understanding the nuances of these policies is essential for ensuring that claims are processed efficiently and accurately.

## GEP25-5.1 - Applicability by date

This policy version applies only to transactions dated in 2025. A later version does not apply retrospectively and an earlier version does not apply to a later transaction.

Interpretive guidance: This clause specifies the applicability of the current version of the policy, indicating that it is only relevant for transactions dated within a specific timeframe. This is crucial for ensuring that employees are aware of which version of the policy governs their claims, thereby preventing confusion and ensuring compliance with the most current rules. In practice, this clause is particularly important during transitional periods when new policies are introduced, as it helps to delineate the boundaries of applicability and reinforces the need for employees to be vigilant about the dates associated with their transactions.

A common misunderstanding is the belief that the latest version of the policy applies retroactively to all past transactions, which is not the case as outlined in this clause. It is essential to recognize that each version of a policy is distinct and only applies to transactions dated within its effective period. This topic is closely related to the order of precedence and circulars, as both emphasize the importance of understanding the temporal context in which policies operate. Misinterpretation of these clauses can lead to improper claims submissions and potential compliance issues, underscoring the importance of clarity in policy communication.

## GEP25-5.2 - Mixed expenses

A bill that combines personal and business items must be itemised. If the business amount cannot be separated from the personal amount, the claim is returned for the business amount rather than approved in full.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to ensure that any expense claim that includes both personal and business items is clearly itemized. This requirement is essential for maintaining transparency and accountability in expense reporting, as it allows for a clear distinction between allowable business expenses and personal expenditures. In situations where a bill cannot be effectively separated into its business and personal components, the clause mandates that the claim be returned for further clarification. This process helps to uphold the integrity of the expense management system and ensures that only legitimate business expenses are approved for reimbursement.

A common misunderstanding is that claims can be approved in full even when they contain mixed expenses, which contradicts the intent of this clause. It is crucial to recognize that itemization is not merely a formality but a necessary step to ensure compliance with policy requirements. This topic is conceptually linked to the principles of insufficient evidence, as both emphasize the importance of clear documentation and verification in the claims process. Proper itemization not only facilitates accurate processing of claims but also reinforces a culture of responsibility and ethical financial practices within the organization.

## GEP25-6.1 - Evidence standard

A decision must cite the applicable clause and every enterprise fact that the clause requires. The travel request is the authoritative source for trip approval and destination; employee narrative never overrides a system record.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish a clear evidentiary standard for decision-making related to travel requests. It ensures that all decisions are grounded in the relevant policy framework and that the travel request serves as the primary document for trip approval and destination details. This clause is particularly relevant in situations where there may be discrepancies between an employee's narrative and the official records, thereby safeguarding the integrity of the approval process and ensuring compliance with established guidelines.

Common misunderstandings regarding this clause may arise from the belief that an employee's narrative can supersede the travel request documentation. It is crucial to recognize that the travel request is the definitive source of information for trip approvals. This clause is conceptually linked to other policy areas, such as documentation standards and accountability measures, reinforcing the importance of accurate record-keeping and adherence to prescribed procedures.
