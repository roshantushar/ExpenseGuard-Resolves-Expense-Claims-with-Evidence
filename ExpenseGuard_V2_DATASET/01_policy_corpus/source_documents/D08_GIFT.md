# Client Gifts and Hospitality Policy

Document ID: D08
Region: GLOBAL
Effective: 2024-01-01 to 2099-12-31
Category: gifts

> Internal policy document of Northstar Analytics Group. Precedence rank 5 (lower rank overrides higher rank where documents differ).

## GIFT-1.1 - Purpose

Modest gifts to external business contacts are reimbursable where they support an existing business relationship and are neither lavish nor intended to influence a decision improperly.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to outline the conditions under which modest gifts to external business contacts may be reimbursed. It is particularly relevant in situations where maintaining or enhancing business relationships is essential for the company's operations. By setting parameters around the appropriateness of gifts, the policy aims to ensure that they are neither extravagant nor intended to improperly influence decisions, thereby safeguarding the company's reputation and ethical standards.

A common misunderstanding may arise regarding what constitutes a 'modest' gift, leading some to believe that any gift, regardless of value, may be acceptable. This clause is interconnected with other policies on ethical behavior and compliance, emphasizing the need for employees to exercise discretion and sound judgment when engaging in gift-giving practices. Clarity in this area helps prevent potential conflicts of interest and reinforces the importance of maintaining professional integrity in business relationships.

## GIFT-1.2 - Prohibited recipients

A gift to a public official, a government-affiliated body or a person who is a decision-maker in an open tender involving the company is prohibited and is rejected regardless of value.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to explicitly prohibit the giving of gifts to certain recipients, including public officials and individuals involved in decision-making processes related to open tenders. This is crucial in maintaining compliance with legal and ethical standards, as it helps to prevent any appearance of impropriety or undue influence. By clearly defining prohibited recipients, the policy safeguards the company against potential legal repercussions and promotes ethical conduct in all business dealings.

A common misunderstanding is that gifts can be given to any recipient as long as they are deemed modest. This clause clarifies that specific individuals and entities are off-limits, regardless of the gift's value. This policy is closely related to other sections concerning compliance and ethical standards, highlighting the importance of understanding the broader implications of gift-giving practices and ensuring that all employees are aware of the restrictions in place to protect the company's integrity.

## GIFT-1.3 - Cash and equivalents

Cash, gift cards, vouchers and other cash equivalents are prohibited as gifts and are rejected.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to prohibit the reimbursement of cash, gift cards, vouchers, and other cash equivalents as gifts. This is essential for maintaining ethical standards and preventing any potential misuse of funds that could arise from the ambiguity surrounding cash-like gifts. By establishing this prohibition, the policy aims to promote transparency and accountability in business practices, ensuring that gifts are tangible and appropriate in nature.

A common misunderstanding may be the belief that cash equivalents can be considered acceptable gifts if they are modest in value. However, this clause clearly delineates that all forms of cash equivalents are prohibited, regardless of their perceived value. This policy is conceptually linked to the broader principles of ethical gifting and compliance, emphasizing the need for clarity and consistency in gift-giving practices to uphold the company's standards and avoid any potential conflicts of interest.

## GIFT-1.4 - Required details

A gift claim must state the recipient name, the recipient organisation and the business purpose. A claim missing any of them is returned for the missing details.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to ensure that all gift claims are substantiated with essential information that validates the business intent behind the gift. By requiring the recipient's name, the recipient's organization, and the business purpose, the company aims to maintain transparency and accountability in its gifting practices. This clause is particularly relevant in situations where employees are recognizing clients, partners, or other stakeholders through gifts, as it helps to ensure that such gestures align with the company's values and compliance requirements.

A common misunderstanding is that the requirement for details is merely administrative, when in fact, it serves a critical role in preventing potential ethical issues related to gifting. Employees may confuse gifts with other forms of business entertainment, which are governed by different policies. This clause is conceptually linked to the Meals and Entertainment Policy, as both deal with the appropriateness of expenditures aimed at fostering business relationships, but they are distinct in their definitions and requirements.

## GIFT-2.1 - Per-gift ceiling

The value of a single gift to one recipient may not exceed the per-gift ceiling in the regional addendum for the transaction date.

Interpretive guidance: This clause establishes a limit on the value of gifts that can be given to a single recipient, thereby promoting responsible and ethical gifting practices within the organization. By adhering to the specified ceiling, employees can ensure that their gift-giving does not create undue influence or perceptions of favoritism. This clause is particularly relevant in scenarios where employees are engaging with clients or partners, as it sets a clear boundary for acceptable gift values that align with the company's ethical standards and compliance protocols.

A frequent misconception is that the ceiling applies uniformly across all contexts, while it actually varies by region and is specified in an addendum. Employees may also confuse this clause with the annual ceiling on gifts to the same recipient organization, which addresses cumulative gifting over a longer period. Understanding this clause in relation to the annual ceiling is crucial, as both aim to regulate gifting practices but do so in different temporal contexts.

## GIFT-2.2 - Annual ceiling

The total of gifts to the same recipient organisation in a calendar year, including the current claim, may not exceed the annual ceiling in the regional addendum. Earlier gifts are found in the previous expense records.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to limit the total value of gifts given to the same recipient organization within a calendar year, thus ensuring that the company's gifting practices remain within ethical boundaries over time. By establishing an annual ceiling, the company seeks to prevent any potential perception of impropriety that may arise from excessive gifting. This clause is particularly relevant in ongoing business relationships where multiple gifts may be offered throughout the year, ensuring that the total value remains reasonable and justifiable.

A common misunderstanding is that the annual ceiling applies only to the current claim, whereas it encompasses all gifts made to the same organization within the specified timeframe. Employees may also confuse this clause with the per-gift ceiling, which governs individual gift values. It is important to recognize that both clauses work together to create a comprehensive framework for managing gifting practices, with one focusing on individual transactions and the other on cumulative totals.

## GIFT-3.1 - Hospitality distinguished

A shared meal is governed by the Meals and Entertainment Policy. A gift is an item given to a recipient to keep.

Interpretive guidance: This clause serves to distinguish between gifts and hospitality, clarifying the nature of each expenditure type within the corporate context. By defining a gift as an item given to a recipient to keep, the company aims to ensure that employees understand the appropriate categorization of their expenses. This distinction is crucial in maintaining compliance with various policies, particularly when engaging in client relations or partnership activities where both gifts and meals may be involved.

A common misunderstanding is that gifts and shared meals can be treated interchangeably, leading to potential misclassification of expenses. Employees may overlook the specific guidelines outlined in the Meals and Entertainment Policy, which governs shared meals, while failing to recognize that gifts have distinct implications for reporting and compliance. Understanding this clause in relation to the Meals and Entertainment Policy is essential for accurate expense reporting and adherence to corporate governance.
