# Travel and Accommodation Policy

Document ID: D04
Region: GLOBAL
Effective: 2024-01-01 to 2099-12-31
Category: travel

> Internal policy document of Northstar Analytics Group. Precedence rank 5 (lower rank overrides higher rank where documents differ).

## TRV-1.1 - Trip authorisation

Hotel and airfare expenses require an approved business trip covering the transaction date, evidenced by the travel request record, unless a valid written exception removes the requirement. A request that is pending, rejected or absent does not evidence approval.

Interpretive guidance: This clause outlines the requirements for trip authorisation, specifically regarding hotel and airfare expenses. It ensures that these expenses are only reimbursed when they are linked to an approved business trip, as evidenced by the travel request record. This is particularly relevant in situations where employees may incur travel-related costs in anticipation of business activities, as it establishes a clear connection between the incurred expenses and the necessary approvals, thereby promoting fiscal responsibility.

A common misunderstanding is that employees may assume expenses can be reimbursed based on verbal confirmations or informal approvals. The clause clearly states that only documented approvals, as indicated in the travel request record, are valid. This principle is closely related to the policies governing travel request statuses, as it reinforces the necessity for formal authorisation processes to ensure that all travel-related expenses are appropriately vetted and aligned with company policy.

## TRV-1.2 - Travel request statuses

A travel request has one of the statuses APPROVED, PENDING or REJECTED. Only APPROVED evidences an authorised trip. A request may reference a conference event identifier and an exception identifier, which are then looked up in their own records.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to define the statuses of travel requests, which are crucial for determining the validity of trip authorisations. It establishes that only requests marked as APPROVED can substantiate an authorised trip, thereby providing a clear framework for employees to understand the implications of their travel request status. This is particularly important in managing expectations and ensuring that employees are aware of the necessary approvals before incurring expenses related to travel.

A common misconception is that a travel request in PENDING status can be treated as an approval for expense reimbursement. However, this clause clarifies that only APPROVED requests are valid for such purposes, thereby preventing potential misuse of the reimbursement process. This topic is interconnected with the policies on trip authorisation and expense reimbursement, as it underscores the importance of adhering to established procedures to maintain the integrity of the expense management system.

## TRV-2.1 - Hotel principles

Hotel accommodation is reimbursable for single occupancy during an approved business trip up to the nightly ceiling for the destination tier and the employee grade band. The employee grade is taken from the employee profile record.

Interpretive guidance: This clause establishes the principles governing hotel accommodation reimbursements during approved business trips. It specifies that reimbursement is applicable for single occupancy and is subject to certain criteria related to the destination and employee grade band. This is particularly relevant in scenarios where employees are required to travel for business purposes, as it provides clear guidelines on what constitutes reimbursable lodging expenses, thereby ensuring consistency and fairness in the reimbursement process.

A common misunderstanding is that employees may assume any hotel expense incurred during a business trip is automatically reimbursable. However, this clause clarifies that reimbursement is contingent upon adherence to specific principles, including occupancy limits and destination-related criteria. This policy is conceptually linked to other travel-related policies, as it emphasizes the importance of aligning expenses with approved travel parameters to uphold the organisation's financial standards and accountability.

## TRV-3.4 - Applying amendments

A percentage amendment to a hotel row is applied to the base ceiling for the transaction year, rounded half up to the rounding unit stated in the circular. An amendment does not compound with another amendment on the same row in the same year.

Interpretive guidance: The policy regarding applying amendments to hotel ceilings is designed to provide flexibility in managing accommodation costs while ensuring that adjustments are systematically applied and clearly communicated. By allowing for percentage amendments to be applied to the base ceiling, the company can respond to changing market conditions and inflation rates, thereby maintaining a relevant and fair approach to travel expenses. This clause is particularly important for ensuring that employees are aware of how their accommodation allowances may fluctuate based on external factors, fostering transparency in expense management.

A common misunderstanding is that amendments can be compounded, leading to confusion about how multiple changes might affect the ceiling for a given year. Employees may mistakenly believe that applying several amendments would result in a higher maximum allowable rate, whereas the policy explicitly states that such compounding is not permitted. This clause is related to the previous policies on nightly hotel ceilings, as it outlines how adjustments to those ceilings will be managed, ensuring that the company maintains control over travel expenses while accommodating necessary changes.

## TRV-4.1 - Long stays

For a stay of more than 14 nights, from 1 January 2025, the ceiling applied to the nightly rate is 90 percent of the ceiling that would otherwise apply.

Interpretive guidance: The provision regarding long stays is intended to address the unique circumstances that arise when employees are required to travel for extended periods. By applying a modified ceiling for accommodations exceeding a specified duration, the policy aims to balance the need for cost-effective lodging with the realities of longer business engagements. This approach not only promotes responsible spending but also recognizes the potential for different pricing structures associated with extended stays, thereby ensuring that employees are supported during their business trips.

A common misconception is that the modified ceiling applies uniformly to all long stays, without consideration of the specific circumstances or location. Employees may not fully understand that the reduced ceiling is designed to reflect the realities of long-term accommodations, which can differ significantly from standard rates. This clause is conceptually linked to the policies on nightly hotel ceilings, as it directly impacts the allowable rates based on the length of stay, ensuring that travel expenses remain within the company's financial parameters.

## TRV-4.2 - Nightly rate

The nightly rate is the total accommodation charge on the bill divided by the number of nights stated on the claim. Incidentals billed separately are claimed under their own categories.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish a clear method for calculating the nightly rate for accommodation expenses incurred during business travel. This calculation is essential in ensuring that reimbursement claims are accurate and align with the company's financial policies. Typical business situations covered by this clause include employees submitting expense reports for hotel stays during business trips, where the total accommodation charge needs to be divided by the number of nights stayed as indicated on the claim. This ensures consistency and fairness in how accommodation expenses are processed.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause is the belief that all charges on a hotel bill are automatically included in the nightly rate calculation. However, it is important to note that only the accommodation charge is considered, while any incidentals must be claimed separately. This clause is conceptually related to other policies regarding travel expenses, particularly those addressing reimbursement limits and exceptions, as it helps delineate which costs are eligible for reimbursement and under what conditions.

## TRV-5.1 - Conference lodging

Where the travel request references an approved conference, the Training and Conferences Policy is consulted before a hotel is rejected solely for exceeding the ordinary ceiling.

Interpretive guidance: This clause serves to clarify the process for lodging expenses associated with approved conferences. It emphasizes the importance of consulting the Training and Conferences Policy when evaluating hotel options, particularly when a hotel exceeds the ordinary ceiling for accommodation costs. This is crucial in ensuring that employees are not unduly penalized for choosing accommodations that may be more suitable for conference attendance, thereby supporting their professional development and networking opportunities.

A frequent misconception is that any hotel exceeding the ordinary ceiling is automatically disqualified for reimbursement. In reality, this clause allows for exceptions when the travel request pertains to an approved conference, thus linking it to the broader context of travel and training policies. This relationship underscores the need for employees to be informed about the various policies that govern travel expenses, particularly those that pertain to professional development and the associated costs.

## TRV-5.2 - Partner hotel uplift

For a registered attendee of an approved conference staying at the official partner hotel named in the conference registry, the nightly ceiling is increased by 20 percent in 2024, 20 percent in 2025 and 25 percent in 2026. The uplift applies after any percentage amendment and before the long-stay factor.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to provide a structured uplift to the nightly ceiling for employees attending approved conferences who choose to stay at designated partner hotels. This uplift recognizes the unique circumstances of conference attendance, where accommodations may be priced higher due to proximity and convenience. It is particularly relevant in situations where employees are required to stay at partner hotels to enhance their conference experience and facilitate networking opportunities.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause is the assumption that the uplift applies universally to all hotel stays. In actuality, it is specifically tied to registered attendees of approved conferences and only applies to partner hotels listed in the conference registry. This clause is interconnected with other policies regarding ceiling exceptions and reimbursement limits, emphasizing the importance of understanding specific conditions under which additional costs may be justified.

## TRV-6.1 - Ceiling exceptions

A nightly rate above the applicable ceiling is reimbursable only through the partner hotel uplift or an approved hotel-limit exception under the Exceptions and Waivers Procedure.

Interpretive guidance: This clause outlines the conditions under which reimbursement for accommodation expenses exceeding the applicable ceiling may be granted. It establishes that such reimbursements are permissible only through the partner hotel uplift or an approved hotel-limit exception. This is crucial for maintaining budgetary control and ensuring that all travel-related expenses align with the company’s financial guidelines while still accommodating necessary exceptions.

A common misconception is that any expense exceeding the ceiling can be reimbursed without justification. However, this clause clearly delineates the specific pathways for reimbursement, which are limited to predefined exceptions. This clause is conceptually linked to the broader framework of expense management and approval processes, highlighting the importance of adhering to established guidelines while also allowing for necessary flexibility in exceptional circumstances.

## TRV-6.2 - Evidence hierarchy

The travel request is the authoritative source for trip approval, destination and linked identifiers. The description written by the employee cannot substitute for a missing record.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish the travel request as the primary source of authority for trip-related approvals and details. This ensures that all travel activities are documented and verifiable, thereby facilitating accurate expense reporting and reimbursement processes. Typical situations covered include instances where employees submit claims for travel expenses and need to reference their travel request to substantiate their claims, ensuring consistency and accountability in expense management.

A common misunderstanding is the belief that any written description provided by the employee can serve as a substitute for the travel request. In reality, the clause emphasizes the necessity of official documentation to support claims. This clause is conceptually related to policies on documentation and record-keeping, reinforcing the need for employees to understand the importance of maintaining accurate records to support their expense claims.

## TRV-7.1 - Personal extension

Cost attributable to a personal extension of a business trip is not reimbursable. Where the comparison with the business-only stay is missing, the reviewer requests it.

Interpretive guidance: This clause specifies that costs associated with personal extensions of business trips are not eligible for reimbursement. This is crucial for delineating between business-related expenses and personal expenditures, ensuring that the company’s financial resources are utilized appropriately. Typical situations this clause addresses include employees who may wish to extend their business travel for personal reasons and need to understand the implications for reimbursement.

A common misunderstanding is that employees may assume that any costs incurred during a personal extension can be claimed. However, this clause clarifies that only expenses directly related to the business purpose of the trip are reimbursable. This topic is conceptually linked to other policies regarding reimbursement eligibility and expense categorization, reinforcing the importance of clear distinctions between business and personal expenses in the context of corporate travel.
