# Ground Transportation Policy

Document ID: D06
Region: GLOBAL
Effective: 2024-01-01 to 2099-12-31
Category: transport

> Internal policy document of Northstar Analytics Group. Precedence rank 5 (lower rank overrides higher rank where documents differ).

## GRD-1.1 - Business ground transport

Taxi and ride-hailing are reimbursable for travel between business locations, an airport, a hotel, a client site or an approved event. The claim must state the origin and the destination.

Interpretive guidance: This clause serves to outline the circumstances under which expenses for taxi and ride-hailing services may be reimbursed. It is designed to facilitate efficient travel between various business-related locations, such as offices, airports, hotels, client sites, or approved events. By specifying the need for a clear origin and destination on claims, the policy ensures that expenses are directly linked to legitimate business activities, thereby promoting accountability and transparency in the reimbursement process.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause is the assumption that all travel expenses incurred during business trips are automatically reimbursable. However, the policy explicitly limits reimbursement to specific types of transport and locations. This clause is conceptually related to others, such as those regarding private vehicle mileage and late-night travel, as they collectively establish a framework for understanding what constitutes reimbursable travel expenses and the documentation required to support such claims.

## GRD-1.2 - Commuting

Ordinary travel between home and the usual place of work is a commute and is not reimbursable, except under the late-night rule.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to clearly delineate the boundaries of reimbursable travel expenses, specifically addressing the distinction between commuting and business travel. Ordinary travel from home to the usual workplace is classified as a commute and is not eligible for reimbursement, which helps to manage the company's financial resources effectively. The exception for late-night travel recognizes that employees may incur additional costs due to extended work hours, thereby providing a measure of support for those situations.

A common misconception is that any travel related to work, including daily commutes, should be reimbursed. However, the policy intends to limit reimbursement to actual business-related travel, thus preventing potential abuse of the reimbursement system. This clause is closely related to the late-night travel policy, as it highlights the specific circumstances under which commuting may be reimbursed, ensuring that employees understand the conditions that apply to different types of travel expenses.

## GRD-2.1 - Late-night travel

Home transport departing after 22:00 is reimbursable where documented business activity ended after 21:30. The claim must state the route and the activity that ended late.

Interpretive guidance: This clause is designed to provide clarity regarding the reimbursement of home transport costs incurred after standard business hours. By stipulating that reimbursement is only available when documented business activities extend beyond a specified time, the policy aims to support employees who may need to travel home late due to work commitments. This provision recognizes the challenges of balancing work demands with personal time, ensuring that employees are not financially penalized for fulfilling their professional responsibilities.

A frequent misunderstanding is that any late-night travel should automatically qualify for reimbursement, regardless of the circumstances. However, the policy requires that the business activity must have concluded after a specified time to be eligible for reimbursement. This clause connects with the commuting policy, as it highlights the distinction between regular travel and exceptional circumstances, ensuring employees are aware of the specific conditions under which late-night travel expenses can be claimed.

## GRD-3.1 - Car rental

Car rental requires manager approval and is available to grade G4 and above.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish the requirements for renting a vehicle for business purposes, emphasizing the necessity of managerial approval and the eligibility criteria based on employee grade. This ensures that car rental expenses are controlled and only incurred when deemed necessary for business operations. By limiting this benefit to certain grades, the policy aims to manage costs while still providing essential resources for employees who require transportation for work-related activities.

A common misconception is that all employees can independently rent vehicles for business purposes without prior approval. However, the requirement for managerial consent is crucial to ensure that such expenses are justified and align with the company's financial policies. This clause is related to other transportation policies, such as those regarding taxi reimbursements and private vehicle mileage, as they collectively form a comprehensive framework for understanding the company's approach to travel-related expenses.

## GRD-4.1 - Private vehicle mileage

Use of a private vehicle is reimbursed at the per-kilometre rate published in the regional addendum multiplied by the distance stated on the claim. A claim for more than the calculated amount cannot be approved as submitted.

Interpretive guidance: This clause outlines the reimbursement policy for employees who utilize their private vehicles for business-related travel. By specifying that reimbursement is calculated based on a per-kilometre rate, the policy ensures that employees are compensated fairly for the costs incurred while using their personal vehicles for work purposes. This approach promotes accountability and ensures that claims are substantiated by actual travel distances, thereby protecting the company's financial interests.

A common misunderstanding is that employees may claim any amount they wish for private vehicle use without adhering to the established calculation method. However, the policy explicitly states that claims exceeding the calculated amount will not be approved, reinforcing the importance of accurate reporting. This clause is conceptually related to other travel reimbursement policies, such as those for taxi services and parking expenses, as they collectively define the parameters for acceptable travel-related claims.

## GRD-5.1 - Parking and tolls

Parking and tolls are reimbursable when incurred on business travel and evidenced by a receipt naming the location.

Interpretive guidance: This clause serves to clarify the conditions under which expenses for parking and tolls incurred during business travel are eligible for reimbursement. By requiring that these expenses be substantiated with a receipt that identifies the location, the policy promotes transparency and accountability in the reimbursement process. This ensures that employees are reimbursed for necessary costs directly related to business activities while discouraging any potential misuse of the reimbursement system.

A common misunderstanding is that all parking and toll expenses are automatically reimbursable regardless of their relevance to business activities. However, the policy specifies that only those incurred during business travel and supported by appropriate documentation will be reimbursed. This clause is related to other travel expense policies, such as those for taxi and private vehicle use, as they collectively establish the framework for what constitutes legitimate business-related travel expenses.
