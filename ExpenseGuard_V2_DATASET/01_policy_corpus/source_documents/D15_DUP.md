# Duplicate, Split and Recurring Expense Controls

Document ID: D15
Region: GLOBAL
Effective: 2024-01-01 to 2099-12-31
Category: controls

> Internal policy document of Northstar Analytics Group. Precedence rank 5 (lower rank overrides higher rank where documents differ).

## DUP-1.1 - Exact duplicate

A claim with the same employee, the same merchant and the same bill number as a reimbursed expense, or the same employee, merchant, transaction date and amount, is an exact duplicate and is rejected.

Interpretive guidance: This clause serves to define what constitutes an exact duplicate claim and establishes the grounds for rejection of such claims. The intent is to prevent the submission of multiple claims for the same expense, thereby safeguarding the company against potential financial discrepancies and ensuring that the reimbursement process remains efficient and transparent. Typical situations covered under this clause include employees who may inadvertently submit duplicate claims for the same expense, either due to oversight or misunderstanding of previous submissions, which could lead to unnecessary complications in the expense approval process.

A common misunderstanding related to this clause is the belief that minor variations in claim details, such as transaction date or amount, can render a claim non-duplicate. Employees may not recognize that specific criteria outlined in the clause, such as the same merchant and bill number, are sufficient to classify a claim as an exact duplicate. This clause is conceptually related to the overall expense submission process, as it emphasizes the importance of accuracy and attention to detail when submitting claims. Understanding this relationship helps employees avoid potential rejections and ensures adherence to corporate expense policies.

## DUP-1.2 - Near duplicate

A claim by the same employee at the same merchant within 3 days of an earlier expense, with an amount within 2 percent of it and a different bill number, is a possible duplicate. The reviewer requests clarification from the employee.

Interpretive guidance: This clause addresses the identification of potential duplicate expense claims submitted by the same employee for the same merchant within a short time frame. The purpose is to ensure that the company maintains accurate financial records and prevents the reimbursement of expenses that may not be legitimate. Typical business situations that this clause covers include instances where an employee may inadvertently submit multiple claims for similar expenses, which could arise from frequent business interactions with the same merchant or a misunderstanding of the expense reporting process.

A common misunderstanding related to this clause is the belief that any expense claim submitted within a short time frame is automatically deemed a duplicate. However, the clause specifies particular conditions, such as the amount being within a defined percentage and the requirement for a different bill number. This concept is closely related to other policies regarding expense approval and verification processes, as it emphasizes the importance of thorough review and clarification to ensure compliance and accuracy in financial reporting.

## DUP-1.3 - Recurring charges

A recurring charge is a repeat at the same merchant by the same employee between 26 and 35 days after an earlier expense whose purpose is recurring, with an amount within 5 percent. It is not a duplicate.

Interpretive guidance: This clause defines recurring charges and outlines the criteria under which they are recognized. The purpose is to differentiate between genuine recurring expenses and potential duplicates, thereby ensuring that employees are reimbursed for legitimate costs associated with ongoing services or products. Typical business situations include subscriptions, memberships, or other regular payments that an employee may incur while conducting business on behalf of the company, which occur within a specified timeframe after the initial expense.

A common misconception is that any repeated expense is automatically classified as a duplicate, leading to unnecessary scrutiny of legitimate recurring charges. This clause clarifies that such charges are not duplicates if they meet specific criteria, thereby distinguishing them from other expense types. This topic is conceptually linked to the broader expense management policies, particularly those addressing approval processes for repeated expenditures, as it highlights the need for clarity and consistency in expense classification.

## DUP-2.1 - Split transactions

Purchases by the same employee at the same merchant for the same project within 3 days of each other, whose amounts differ by more than 2 percent, are treated together. The combined amount governs the approval threshold.

Interpretive guidance: This clause addresses the treatment of split transactions, which occur when an employee makes multiple purchases at the same merchant for the same project within a short period. The purpose is to ensure that these transactions are considered collectively for approval purposes, thereby streamlining the expense review process. Common business scenarios include situations where an employee may need to make several purchases to complete a project, necessitating separate transactions that, when combined, may exceed normal approval thresholds.

A frequent misunderstanding is that split transactions are treated as entirely separate claims, which can lead to confusion in the approval process. However, this clause clarifies that such transactions must be evaluated together, reflecting their combined impact on the approval threshold. This concept is closely related to other policies regarding expense approval and reporting, emphasizing the importance of recognizing the cumulative nature of expenses to maintain financial integrity and oversight.

## DUP-2.2 - Approval for combined spend

Where the combined amount crosses an approval threshold that each purchase alone did not, an approval of type COMBINED_SPEND is required. If none exists the reviewer requests it. If an approval exists but of another type, the evidence conflicts and the claim is escalated.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish the requirement for combined spend approval when the total amount of split transactions exceeds a specified approval threshold. This ensures that all expenses, even if submitted separately, are subject to the necessary scrutiny and authorization. Typical situations this clause covers include instances where an employee makes multiple purchases that, when aggregated, necessitate a higher level of approval to ensure compliance with fiscal responsibility and governance standards.

A common misconception is that individual purchases do not require any form of approval if they fall below the threshold, leading to potential oversight of larger combined expenses. This clause clarifies that even if individual amounts are within limits, their aggregation may trigger additional approval requirements. This topic is conceptually linked to the broader expense approval framework, highlighting the importance of comprehensive oversight in ensuring that all expenditures align with corporate policy and budgetary constraints.
