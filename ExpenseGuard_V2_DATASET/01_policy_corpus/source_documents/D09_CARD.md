# Corporate Card and Personal Spend Policy

Document ID: D09
Region: GLOBAL
Effective: 2024-01-01 to 2099-12-31
Category: card

> Internal policy document of Northstar Analytics Group. Precedence rank 5 (lower rank overrides higher rank where documents differ).

## CARD-1.1 - Personal spend

Purely personal purchases are not reimbursable even when paid with a corporate card.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to delineate the boundaries of reimbursable expenses, explicitly stating that personal purchases made with a corporate card are not eligible for reimbursement. This policy is essential for maintaining fiscal responsibility and ensuring that corporate resources are used solely for business-related expenses. It is particularly relevant in scenarios where employees may inadvertently use corporate funds for personal items, as it reinforces the expectation of separating personal and professional financial transactions.

A common misunderstanding is that employees may believe that any purchase made with a corporate card, regardless of its nature, could be reimbursed under certain circumstances. This misconception can lead to confusion and potential misuse of corporate resources. The clause is conceptually related to the accidental personal use policy, which addresses instances where personal purchases occur unintentionally, but this clause sets a clear prohibition on personal spending regardless of intent.

## CARD-1.2 - Accidental personal use

An accidental personal purchase on a corporate card is rejected for reimbursement and handled by the repayment process outside expense review.

Interpretive guidance: This clause addresses the handling of accidental personal purchases made on a corporate card, outlining that such transactions are not eligible for reimbursement and must be managed through a repayment process. This policy is designed to protect the integrity of corporate funds while providing a clear protocol for employees who may inadvertently make personal purchases. It is particularly relevant in situations where employees may mix personal and business expenses, emphasizing the need for vigilance in expense management.

A common misunderstanding is that accidental personal purchases could be reimbursed if they are reported promptly, which is not the case according to this clause. Employees may also confuse this policy with the prohibition on personal spending, not recognizing that this clause specifically addresses unintentional errors. Understanding the distinction between intentional personal purchases and accidental use is vital for compliance with corporate policies and maintaining accurate financial records.

## CARD-2.1 - Consumer services

Streaming, gaming, personal fitness, household utilities and other consumer services are presumed personal unless a category policy names the specific business use.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to delineate the types of consumer services that are generally considered personal expenses. In typical business situations, employees may encounter scenarios where they seek reimbursement for expenses related to streaming services, gaming subscriptions, personal fitness memberships, and household utilities. This clause serves as a guideline to help employees understand that unless explicitly stated in a category policy, such expenses are not reimbursable and should be classified as personal.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause is the assumption that all consumer services can be justified as business-related if they are used for work purposes. However, the clause clearly indicates that only those services explicitly named in a category policy can be deemed acceptable for reimbursement. This topic is closely related to the broader expense classification policies, as it emphasizes the importance of understanding what constitutes a personal versus a business expense, thereby aiding in compliance with the overall expense management framework.

## CARD-3.1 - Merchant risk classes

Each merchant in the merchant directory carries a risk class. STANDARD merchants are ordinary. RESTRICTED_PROHIBITED merchants, such as casinos and adult entertainment venues, are not reimbursable and the claim is rejected. RESTRICTED_REVIEW merchants, such as prepaid-card resellers, require Finance review and the claim is escalated.

Interpretive guidance: This clause establishes the classification of merchants within the company's merchant directory, which is essential for determining the reimbursement eligibility of expenses incurred at various establishments. In typical business situations, employees may encounter a wide range of merchants when making purchases for company-related activities. The risk class assigned to each merchant helps to streamline the review process for expense claims and ensures that employees are aware of which merchants fall under standard, restricted, or prohibited categories.

A common misunderstanding about this clause is the belief that all merchants can be reimbursed as long as they are used for business purposes. However, the clause specifies that certain merchants, particularly those classified as restricted or prohibited, will not be reimbursed. This classification is conceptually linked to the broader topic of expense approval processes, as it underscores the necessity of adhering to established merchant guidelines to avoid potential claim rejections and maintain compliance with the company's risk management policies.

## CARD-4.1 - Merchant lookup

The merchant risk class is taken from the merchant directory, not from the merchant name as typed on the claim.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to clarify that the merchant risk class is determined by the official merchant directory rather than the name entered by the employee on the expense claim. This is particularly important in business situations where employees may inadvertently misclassify merchants due to typographical errors or misunderstandings of merchant names. By reinforcing the reliance on the merchant directory, this clause aims to promote consistency and accuracy in the expense reimbursement process.

A common misunderstanding related to this clause is the assumption that the name of the merchant as typed on the claim is sufficient for determining reimbursement eligibility. Employees may mistakenly believe that they can override or ignore the merchant directory's classifications. This topic relates to the broader expense management policies, as it highlights the importance of utilizing official resources and maintaining accurate records to ensure compliance and facilitate smoother reimbursement processes.
