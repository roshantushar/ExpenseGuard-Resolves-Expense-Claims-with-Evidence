# Approval Authority, Delegation and Budget Control Policy

Document ID: D10
Region: GLOBAL
Effective: 2024-01-01 to 2099-12-31
Category: approval

> Internal policy document of Northstar Analytics Group. Precedence rank 5 (lower rank overrides higher rank where documents differ).

## APR-1.1 - Manager approval

A single discretionary expense above SGD 500 equivalent requires manager approval. The regional addenda may set a lower local threshold, which then controls.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish the requirement for manager approval for discretionary expenses that exceed a specified threshold. This clause is particularly relevant in business situations where employees may incur significant expenses that require oversight to ensure that spending aligns with company policies and budgetary constraints. By mandating manager approval, the company aims to promote fiscal responsibility and accountability within its expense management framework.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause is the belief that all expenses require manager approval, regardless of their nature or amount. However, the clause specifically applies to discretionary expenses above a certain threshold, indicating that not all transactions necessitate managerial oversight. This topic is conceptually related to other approval processes within the expense policy, as it emphasizes the need for a structured review mechanism to maintain control over company expenditures while allowing for flexibility in lower-value transactions.

## APR-1.2 - Director approval

A single discretionary expense above SGD 1,500 equivalent requires director approval for transactions dated in 2024 and 2025, and above SGD 2,000 equivalent for transactions dated in 2026.

Interpretive guidance: This clause outlines the requirement for director approval for discretionary expenses that exceed a specified threshold, particularly for transactions occurring in designated years. This is essential in business situations where employees may need to make larger purchases that could significantly impact the company's financial standing. By requiring director approval, the company ensures that higher-value expenditures are scrutinized and justified, thereby promoting responsible spending practices.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause is the assumption that director approval is required for all discretionary expenses, regardless of their amount or timing. In reality, this clause delineates specific thresholds that trigger the need for such approval. This topic is closely related to the broader approval processes outlined in the expense policy, as it highlights the importance of tiered oversight based on the value and timing of expenses, ensuring that appropriate levels of management are involved in significant financial decisions.

## APR-1.3 - Finance review

A single discretionary expense above SGD 5,000 equivalent requires Finance review and is escalated. An exception to a prohibited category or a conflict between an approval and the transaction facts also requires Finance review.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to stipulate that discretionary expenses exceeding a specified threshold require Finance review, which serves to ensure that such expenses are in compliance with company policies and financial guidelines. This is particularly relevant in business situations where employees may submit claims for substantial expenditures that necessitate additional scrutiny. By involving the Finance department, the company aims to mitigate financial risks and ensure that all claims are valid and justifiable.

A common misunderstanding related to this clause is the belief that Finance review is only necessary for exceptionally high-value expenses. However, the clause also indicates that exceptions to prohibited categories or conflicts between approvals and transaction facts will also trigger a Finance review. This topic is conceptually linked to the overall expense approval hierarchy, as it reinforces the importance of thorough review processes to uphold financial integrity and compliance across all levels of expense management.

## APR-2.1 - Approval validity

An approval is valid only if its status is APPROVED, it names the employee, its type covers the expense category, it covers the transaction date, and the approver level is at least the level the amount requires. Approval types are GENERAL, TRAVEL, SOFTWARE, ENTERTAINMENT, TRAINING and COMBINED_SPEND.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish the criteria under which an approval for expenses is deemed valid. It outlines the essential components that must be present for an approval to be recognized, including the approval status, the identification of the employee, the relevant expense category, the transaction date, and the appropriate approver level. This clause is particularly pertinent in scenarios where expenses are being submitted for reimbursement or pre-approval, ensuring that all necessary checks are in place to maintain financial integrity and compliance within the organization.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause is the belief that any form of approval is sufficient, regardless of its completeness or relevance to the specific expense. This misconception can lead to complications in the expense approval process, as approvals that do not meet the outlined criteria may result in delays or rejections. This clause is closely related to the approval scope, as both emphasize the importance of specificity in approvals, ensuring that each approval is tailored to the unique circumstances of the expense in question.

## APR-2.2 - Approval scope

An approval cannot be reused for another employee, another category or another date range unless it says so. An approval whose type does not cover the expense is treated as conflicting evidence.

Interpretive guidance: This clause serves to clarify the limitations on the reuse of approvals, emphasizing that each approval is specific to an employee, expense category, and date range unless explicitly stated otherwise. This specificity is crucial in maintaining accountability and traceability within the expense management process. It is particularly relevant in situations where multiple employees may incur similar expenses or when expenses are submitted over a period of time, ensuring that each approval is appropriately linked to its corresponding transaction.

A frequent misconception is that approvals can be generically applied across different employees or expense categories, which can result in compliance issues and financial discrepancies. Understanding this clause is essential, as it relates to the approval validity, reinforcing the need for each approval to be distinctly applicable to its designated context. This interconnectedness ensures that the approval process is both rigorous and transparent, safeguarding the organization against potential misuse of approvals.

## APR-3.1 - Missing approval

Where an approval is required and no valid approval record exists, the reviewer requests it from the employee. Where the amount requires Finance review, or the approval evidence conflicts, the claim is escalated instead.

Interpretive guidance: The intent of this clause is to outline the procedure to follow when an approval is required but not present. It emphasizes the responsibility of the reviewer to request the necessary approval from the employee, thereby ensuring that all expenses are properly authorized before processing. This clause is particularly relevant in scenarios where expenses are submitted without prior approval, underscoring the importance of compliance with the organization's expense policy and maintaining financial oversight.

One common misunderstanding is that expenses can be processed without any form of approval, leading to potential financial irregularities. This clause is crucial in differentiating between valid and invalid claims, as it establishes a clear protocol for handling missing approvals. It is conceptually linked to the invalid delegation clause, as both address situations where proper authorization is lacking, highlighting the need for rigorous adherence to approval processes to ensure fiscal responsibility.

## APR-4.1 - Delegation

A manager who is unavailable may delegate approval authority. The approval record then has the status DELEGATED and references a delegation record. The delegation is valid only on dates inside its validity period and only for amounts up to its stated SGD limit, and the delegate must hold the approver level the amount requires.

Interpretive guidance: This clause delineates the conditions under which a manager may delegate their approval authority in instances of unavailability. It specifies that the delegation must be documented and that the delegated authority is bound by certain parameters, including validity periods and approver levels. This provision is particularly useful in maintaining continuity in the approval process, ensuring that expenses can still be authorized in the absence of the primary approver, thereby facilitating timely processing of claims.

A common misunderstanding is that delegation can occur without adherence to the specified conditions, which may lead to unauthorized approvals or financial discrepancies. This clause is closely related to the invalid delegation clause, as both address the importance of maintaining the integrity of the approval process through proper delegation practices. Understanding the nuances of delegation is essential for managers to ensure compliance and uphold the standards set forth in the expense policy.

## APR-4.2 - Invalid delegation

If the delegation is outside its validity period, the reviewer requests a valid approval. If the delegation is valid but the amount exceeds its limit or the delegate holds a lower level than the amount requires, the claim is escalated.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish the protocol for handling invalid delegations of approval authority. It outlines the actions that must be taken when a delegation falls outside its validity period or does not meet the necessary criteria, such as exceeding the stated limit or involving a delegate with a lower approver level. This clause is critical in ensuring that the delegation process remains robust and that all approvals are valid and compliant with the organization's policies.

A common misconception is that any delegated approval is automatically valid, regardless of compliance with the established criteria. This misunderstanding can lead to unauthorized claims being processed, which may result in financial discrepancies. This clause is conceptually linked to the delegation clause, as both emphasize the importance of adhering to the parameters set for delegation. Together, they ensure that the approval process remains transparent and accountable, safeguarding the organization's financial integrity.

## APR-5.1 - Cost-centre budget

Where a claim is charged to a cost centre whose status is FROZEN, any expense above SGD 200 equivalent is escalated to Finance. Where the remaining budget of an OPEN cost centre is smaller than the claim amount, the reviewer requests budget-owner approval.

Interpretive guidance: This clause is designed to address the implications of charging expenses to cost centres with different statuses, specifically focusing on frozen and open cost centres. It establishes a clear protocol for escalating claims that exceed certain thresholds or when the remaining budget is insufficient. This is particularly important for maintaining budgetary control and ensuring that expenses are managed within the constraints of the organization's financial framework.

A common misunderstanding is that all expenses can be charged to any cost centre without regard for its status, which can lead to budget overruns and financial mismanagement. This clause is closely related to the approval scope, as both highlight the need for careful consideration of the financial implications of expenses. Understanding this clause is essential for employees to navigate the expense submission process effectively, ensuring that all claims are compliant with budgetary guidelines.

## APR-5.2 - Project hierarchy

A project that has a parent project is charged to the cost centre of the parent project. The parent project, if any, is looked up in the project registry.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish a clear framework for the allocation of costs associated with projects that have a hierarchical structure. In typical business scenarios, this clause applies when a project is initiated under the umbrella of a parent project, ensuring that all expenses are accurately charged to the appropriate cost centre. This facilitates effective budget management and financial reporting, as it allows for a consolidated view of project expenditures and resource allocation within the organization. By maintaining this hierarchy, the company can better track the financial performance of both parent and child projects, enabling informed decision-making and strategic planning.

Common misunderstandings regarding this clause may arise from confusion about the relationship between parent and child projects. Some employees might assume that costs can be allocated arbitrarily or that they can charge expenses to their own cost centres without regard to the established hierarchy. It is essential to clarify that this clause is closely related to other financial policies, such as budget management and cost allocation, which emphasize the importance of accurate reporting and accountability. Understanding this connection can help employees recognize the significance of adhering to the project hierarchy in maintaining the integrity of financial records.
