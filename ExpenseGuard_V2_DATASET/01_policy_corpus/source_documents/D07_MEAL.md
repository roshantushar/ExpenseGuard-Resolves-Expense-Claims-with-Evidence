# Meals and Entertainment Policy

Document ID: D07
Region: GLOBAL
Effective: 2024-01-01 to 2099-12-31
Category: meals

> Internal policy document of Northstar Analytics Group. Precedence rank 5 (lower rank overrides higher rank where documents differ).

## MEAL-1.1 - Employee meals

A meal consumed only by employees during ordinary work is reimbursable up to the per-person employee meal ceiling in the regional addendum and requires a business reason beyond routine attendance. The ceiling is applied to the total divided by the number of attendees.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to establish guidelines for the reimbursement of meals consumed solely by employees during regular work hours. This provision is particularly relevant in situations where employees engage in business meetings or discussions over meals, which may enhance productivity and collaboration. It is designed to ensure that the company supports its workforce while maintaining fiscal responsibility. The requirement for a business reason beyond routine attendance emphasizes the need for meals to serve a specific business purpose rather than merely providing sustenance during work hours.

Common misunderstandings regarding this clause may arise from the interpretation of what constitutes a 'business reason.' Employees might assume that any meal taken during work hours qualifies for reimbursement, but the clause clearly stipulates that there must be a defined business purpose. This topic is conceptually linked to the broader expense policy, particularly in relation to client meals and business entertainment, where similar principles of purpose and attendee classification apply, ensuring a consistent approach to reimbursement across different types of meals.

## MEAL-1.2 - Client meals

A meal with at least one external attendee is a client meal. The claim must name the external attendees or their organisations and the business purpose. Per-attendee spend is the total divided by all attendees present, including employees, and is compared with the regional client meal ceiling.

Interpretive guidance: This clause outlines the criteria for classifying meals that include external attendees as client meals. It is particularly pertinent in scenarios where employees engage with clients or business partners over meals to foster relationships or discuss business matters. By requiring the identification of external attendees and the business purpose of the meal, the policy aims to ensure that expenditures are justifiable and aligned with the company’s objectives. This structure not only promotes transparency but also encourages employees to be intentional in their client interactions.

A common misconception surrounding this clause is the belief that any meal with external attendees is automatically reimbursable without further justification. However, the requirement to specify attendees and the business purpose is crucial for compliance and accountability. This clause is closely related to the employee meals policy, as both deal with meal reimbursements but differ in terms of attendee classification and the associated ceilings, reinforcing the need for clarity in expense claims.

## MEAL-1.3 - Business entertainment

External entertainment that is primarily recreational is tested against the per-external-attendee entertainment ceiling in the regional addendum, not the client meal ceiling, and requires manager approval above the approval threshold for entertainment.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to delineate the parameters for reimbursing expenses related to external entertainment that is primarily recreational in nature. This is relevant in situations where employees host clients or partners in a social setting, aiming to strengthen business relationships. By specifying that such entertainment must adhere to a separate ceiling and requires manager approval, the clause ensures that expenditures are appropriate and aligned with the company's strategic goals while maintaining fiscal oversight.

Common misunderstandings may arise from the interpretation of what constitutes 'primarily recreational' entertainment. Employees might mistakenly believe that any social gathering qualifies for reimbursement, but the clause emphasizes the need for a clear distinction between business-related meals and entertainment. This topic is conceptually linked to client meals and employee meals, as all these categories involve external interactions but are governed by different reimbursement criteria and approval processes, highlighting the importance of adhering to the specific guidelines for each type.

## MEAL-2.1 - Alcohol

Alcohol follows the regional addendum. Where permitted, it must be incidental to an otherwise reimbursable client meal or event and its share of the bill is limited as the addendum states.

Interpretive guidance: This clause governs the reimbursement of alcohol expenses, stipulating that such costs must be incidental to a reimbursable client meal or event. This is particularly relevant in contexts where alcohol is served during business meetings or client dinners, providing an opportunity for relationship-building in a more relaxed atmosphere. By aligning alcohol expenses with the overall meal or event reimbursement, the policy aims to ensure that these costs are reasonable and justifiable within the broader context of business expenditures.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause is the assumption that alcohol can be reimbursed independently of the meal or event. However, the clause clearly states that alcohol must be incidental, which reinforces the need for it to be part of a larger business purpose. This topic is related to the broader categories of client meals and business entertainment, as all these areas involve considerations of appropriateness and justification for expenses, emphasizing the importance of maintaining a clear connection between the type of expense and its business relevance.

## MEAL-2.2 - Tips and service charges

Mandatory service charges are reimbursable. A voluntary gratuity above 15 percent of the pre-tip bill requires manager approval unless the regional addendum is stricter.

Interpretive guidance: This clause addresses the reimbursement of tips and service charges associated with meals and events. It is particularly relevant in situations where employees dine at restaurants or venues that include mandatory service charges or where gratuities are customary. By allowing reimbursement of mandatory charges while requiring approval for voluntary gratuities above a specified threshold, the policy seeks to balance employee discretion with fiscal responsibility, ensuring that expenses remain reasonable and justifiable.

Common misunderstandings may arise regarding the distinction between mandatory service charges and voluntary gratuities. Employees might believe that all tips are reimbursable without consideration of the circumstances, but the clause makes it clear that only mandatory charges qualify without additional approval. This topic is closely related to other meal-related policies, such as those governing client meals and employee meals, as they all require a thoughtful approach to expense claims, ensuring that reimbursements reflect the company’s commitment to responsible spending.

## MEAL-3.1 - Default ceilings for locations without an addendum

For a country with no regional addendum the employee meal ceiling is SGD 60 per person and the client meal ceiling is SGD 110 per attendee. These defaults never apply to Singapore, India or Japan.

Interpretive guidance: This clause establishes default ceilings for meal reimbursements in locations without a regional addendum, providing a baseline for employee and client meal expenditures. It is particularly relevant in situations where employees travel to countries lacking specific guidelines, ensuring that there is a clear standard for reimbursement. By defining these defaults, the policy aims to facilitate consistent expense management across diverse geographical locations while maintaining a focus on fiscal responsibility.

A common misunderstanding may arise from the belief that these default ceilings apply universally, regardless of the specific regional context. However, the clause explicitly states that they do not apply to certain countries, which is crucial for compliance. This topic is conceptually linked to the broader expense policy framework, particularly in relation to regional addendums and the varying reimbursement standards that may exist, highlighting the importance of understanding the specific guidelines applicable to each location.

## MEAL-3.2 - Attendee evidence

If the attendee count is needed for a per-person test and cannot be established from the claim, the reviewer requests it.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to ensure that the necessary attendee count is accurately established when evaluating claims related to meals. This is particularly relevant in situations where the reimbursement is contingent upon a per-person test, such as when determining the appropriateness of meal expenses for group events. By requiring the reviewer to request attendee evidence when it is not provided, the policy aims to maintain transparency and accountability in expense reporting, ensuring that claims are substantiated with clear documentation.

A common misunderstanding regarding this clause is the assumption that any claim can be approved without sufficient evidence of attendee counts. Some may believe that the burden of proof lies solely with the claimant, whereas the policy clearly delineates the reviewer's responsibility to seek clarification when necessary. This clause is conceptually linked to neighboring policies concerning documentation and expense verification, highlighting the importance of accurate record-keeping and the role of reviewers in maintaining compliance with the overall expense policy.

## MEAL-4.1 - Mixed-purpose events

Where an event combines employee-only and external activity and the allocation is unclear, the reviewer requests clarification instead of choosing the more generous ceiling.

Interpretive guidance: This clause serves to clarify the treatment of mixed-purpose events, where both employee and external activities occur. It is essential in situations where the allocation of expenses between these two categories is ambiguous, as it prevents the reviewer from making assumptions that could lead to inappropriate reimbursements. By requiring clarification instead of defaulting to more generous allowances, the policy promotes fairness and ensures that expenses are allocated correctly according to their intended purpose.

A frequent misconception is that reviewers can arbitrarily decide on the allocation of expenses based on their discretion. This can lead to inconsistencies in how similar events are treated. This clause is closely related to the principles of expense justification and proper allocation found in other sections of the policy, reinforcing the need for clear communication and documentation when expenses involve multiple purposes to avoid potential conflicts of interest.

## MEAL-5.1 - Meals during conferences

Meals included in a conference fee cannot also be claimed separately. Where inclusion is unclear the reviewer requests the conference programme.

Interpretive guidance: The purpose of this clause is to prevent double-dipping in expense claims related to meals during conferences. It addresses scenarios where meals are included in the conference fee, thus ensuring that employees do not claim reimbursement for these meals separately. This policy is vital for maintaining fiscal responsibility and integrity in expense reporting, as it encourages employees to be mindful of what has already been covered by the conference registration, thereby avoiding unnecessary expenditures.

A common misunderstanding is that employees may believe they can claim separate meal expenses if they are unsure about the inclusion of meals in the conference fee. This could lead to confusion and potential violations of the policy. This clause is conceptually linked to other policies regarding reimbursement eligibility and documentation requirements, emphasizing the importance of clarity in expense claims and the need for employees to verify what is included in their conference fees before submitting claims.
